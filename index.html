FRISA Aerospace

Mandrel fatigue failure

Team project in the course Failure Analysis and Prevention, Tecnológico de Monterrey

Role
Root-cause investigation
Period
05/2026 – 06/2026
Tools
Ansys FEA, metallography, SEM

FEA predicted 49,500 cycles under ideal static loading. In service, the mandrel failed after 30 forgings.

Investigated why a 4340 steel ring-rolling mandrel failed far sooner than an ideal static-load FEA predicted. The team combined FEA fatigue-life estimation, fracture mechanics, Rockwell and Vickers hardness profiling, metallography (2% nital etch) and SEM fractography.

  • SEM fractography showed chevron marks, ratchet marks and a large instantaneous-fracture zone, consistent with low-cycle rotating-bending fatigue from multiple crack origins.
  • The gap between predicted and real life points to pre-existing surface or machining defects as the main driver of failure.
  • Hardened case 16 mm deep, 420 HV tempered martensite.
Recommended
  • Ultrasonic crack inspection as predictive maintenance
  • Laser-ultrasonic assisted ball burnishing (LUAB) of the mandrel surface
  • Mandrel life tracking under 5S
03 / 07

Daikin

Coupling failure and redesign

Team project in the course Machine Element Design, Tecnológico de Monterrey

Role
FEA, modal analysis and redesign
Period
01/2026 – 04/2026
Tools
Ansys static and modal FEA

In simulation, the redesign raised the first natural frequency to 118 Hz (original assembly: 12.6–46.8 Hz), with the assembly still under 55 kg.

A motor-to-shaft coupling in a Daikin ventilation unit kept failing in the field. Static FEA ruled out overstress. A modal analysis pointed to resonance near the first natural frequency, which loosens the motor-mount bolts, misaligns the shaft and eventually shears the coupling.

  • A 5-whys analysis traced each failure symptom back to resonance near the first bending mode.
  • Replacing the insulating foam in the housing with structural ABS foam raised the stiffness, and with it the natural frequency.
Recommended
  • Calibrated-torque preload on motor-mount and set-screw bolts to prevent slip under vibration

Heat transfer and pressure drop compared for three turbulator configurations (no insert, 116 mm coil, 165 mm coil).

Psychrometric chamber CFD

Team project in the course Design of Thermofluidic Systems, Tecnológico de Monterrey

Role
CFD simulation
Period
11/2025 – 12/2025
Tools
Ansys Fluent, laminar and k‑ε, 0.84 M elements

A filleted nozzle inlet lowered peak velocity by about 4% in simulation.

Simulated the airflow inside a psychrometric test chamber for HVAC units of two sizes, 5 TR and 25 TR (about 17.6 kW and 87.9 kW of cooling capacity). The model mapped the flow around the inlet nozzles and diffusers and compared laminar with turbulent (k‑ε) solutions.

  • High vorticity and recirculation appeared at sharp inlet and outlet turns and around the diffuser nozzles in both chassis sizes.
  • The fillet was proposed as a geometry-only change at the nozzle inlet.
06 / 07

Ternium

Sheet-steel formability FEM

Team project in the course Design of Products under Static Loads, Tecnológico de Monterrey

Role
Explicit FEM simulation
Period
02/2025 – 06/2025
Tools
Ansys LS-DYNA

The simulated forming-limit curve reaches its minimum near plane strain, at a major strain of 0.19.

Built an explicit-dynamics FEM model of the Nakajima test, a hemispherical punch stretching a clamped steel sheet, to predict sheet-metal formability for Ternium. Seven specimen widths (geometries A to G) were simulated in the ISO 12004-2 test setup to trace a forming-limit curve (FLC).

  • Each geometry gave one pair of major and minor strains just before fracture, which is one point of the curve.
  • Fracture onset was located by tracking the safety-factor field frame by frame.
  • At first predicted fracture, geometry A reached 29.6 mm of total deformation.
Known limitation: the curve is only partly validThe model used generic isotropic structural steel instead of Ternium’s CR420 with its real hardening and anisotropy, and failure was read from a stress-based safety factor, not from the onset of necking (strain localization) that ISO 12004-2 uses. That is why the right-hand branch falls again for the widest specimens (0.29 → 0.26), which a real FLC does not do. Next step: CR420 material data, a localization-based failure criterion and validation against a tensile test.

A single-stage planetary set built from library gears, printed and reprinted until it turned freely by hand.

Built a single-stage planetary gear train with a ring gear, a sun gear, three equally spaced planets and a carrier plate. The gear shapes came from the SolidWorks Toolbox library, and the carrier, bores and clearances were designed around them for FDM printing.

  • The three planets mesh with the sun and the ring at the same time, which makes the fit sensitive to printing tolerances.
  • The first print was too tight to rotate, so the bore fits and tooth clearances were adjusted and the parts reprinted.
